What it does

Daylook watches which app and window you're actually working in, and quietly turns that into a readable timeline — not “Chrome: 2h,” but “Research on JWT rotation, then a long PR review.” Ask it about today, look back on past days, or tell it to ignore an app. Everything stays on your Mac; screenshots never leave your machine except to be summarized, and nothing is shared without you.

Installing

  1. Download & open the disk image

    Open Daylook.dmg from your Downloads, then drag Daylook onto the Applications folder in the window that appears.

  2. Open it the first time

    Open Daylook from your Applications folder. Because it's a small independent app (not from the App Store), macOS won't open it on the first try — that's expected.

    macOS Sequoia: if you see “Daylook can't be opened,” go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click “Open Anyway.” Confirm once, and it opens normally forever after. (The old right-click → Open trick no longer works on Sequoia.)

  3. Enter your invite code

    Daylook opens in your browser with a setup box. Paste the invite code you were given (it looks like cl_…) and click Connect. This is what lets the app use its AI. It's stored only on your Mac.

  4. Allow screen recording (only when you start tracking)

    The first time you say “start tracking my screen,” macOS asks for Screen Recording permission — Daylook needs it to see which window is in front. Turn on Daylook under System Settings → Privacy & Security → Screen Recording, then start tracking again.

    Nothing is recorded until you ask it to track, and you can stop any time by saying “stop tracking.”

Good to know

Is my screen data private?

Yes. Capture only runs when you turn it on. Screenshots live on your Mac; only what's needed to summarize a stretch of work is sent to the AI, and the plain-language journal is saved locally.

Which Macs are supported?

Apple Silicon (M1 through M4) on macOS 12 or newer. An Intel build isn't available in this version.

How do I uninstall it?

Quit Daylook and drag it from Applications to the Trash. To remove your journal too, delete the ~/.daylook folder in your home directory.

Why does it open in a browser?

Daylook runs a tiny local server on your Mac and uses your browser as its window — it never connects your browser to the internet for this. The address is always 127.0.0.1 (your own machine).
